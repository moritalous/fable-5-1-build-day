## What it does

- **Head tracking**: estimates head position, yaw and tilt (roll and pitch) from frame differences in the webcam image
- **Vowel detection**: runs an FFT on the microphone input and uses the first two formants to decide which of あ, い, う, え and お is being spoken. The avatar's mouth takes that shape
- Tracking panel: yaw, pitch and roll in degrees, a blink counter, the detected vowel with its confidence, the input level, the tracking latency in milliseconds, and the frame rate
- **Vowel chart**: plots the first and second formants as a point. The five vowel regions are marked, so you can see the point land in the right region as you speak
- Your mirrored camera view with the tracking drawn over it
- The character is drawn entirely in code (no images). Hair and clothing swing with physics, and it breathes and blinks on its own when you hold still
- Stage lighting, a scrolling comment stream, and a reaction effect that fires when you open your mouth wide or tilt your head hard
- Switchable character designs and backgrounds

## The prompt

```text wrap
Create a VTuber studio that turns whoever sits in front of the laptop into an animated character on a virtual stage, stage-worthy and readable from across a room, with the avatar's head, eyes and mouth following the real person in real time.

Use the webcam to track the head — its position, how far it is turned and how far it is tilted — by comparing successive frames and analysing the image yourself, and use the microphone to drive the mouth: run your own FFT, find the first two formants, and decide which of the five Japanese vowels is being spoken, so the character's mouth actually forms あ, い, う, え or お rather than simply opening and closing with the volume.

Beside the stage show a live tracking panel: head yaw, pitch and roll in degrees, a blink counter, the detected vowel with your confidence in it, the input level, the tracking latency in milliseconds and the frame rate; also plot the first and second formants as a moving point on a vowel chart with the five Japanese vowel regions marked, so I can watch the point land in the right region as I speak, and show my mirrored webcam view with the tracking drawn over it.

Draw the character entirely in code — no images, no external assets — as a soft two-dimensional avatar whose hair and clothing swing with a little physics as the head moves, breathing and blinking on its own when I hold still, and give me a stage with lighting, a comment stream scrolling across the screen, and a reaction effect that fires when I open my mouth wide or tilt my head hard. Let me switch between two or three character designs and change the background. Build it as one self-contained HTML file with no external libraries or assets, and keep it at 60 fps so the avatar never feels laggy.
```
